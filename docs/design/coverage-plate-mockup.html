Legend

Surveyed
Drawn from the company's own data. True by construction — the answer re-reads the live rows, so it cannot go stale between readings.
Attested
Extracted from something someone wrote, then approved. A named colleague stood behind it and is on the record.
On the record
Raw source material, unedited. Not a claim about what is true — what was actually said. Testimony, not fact.
In dispute
Two claims contradict and both stand, attributed. Atlas has not picked, and will not.
Unsurveyed
No source connected, or nothing extracted yet. Drawn rather than omitted. Set in italic throughout, after the chart convention.

Notes

    Why not a graph

    A force-directed graph renders topology, which nobody asks about, and becomes a hairball at scale. Coverage is the thesis, so the plate renders area surveyed — and draws the unsurveyed ground instead of omitting it.

    The empty state is the pitch

    Every rival demo opens on a dense, impressive graph. Switch to Day one: three lit quads and a mostly blank sheet. That is honest, it is interesting, and it is true with ten facts — which is what a real first week looks like.

    Fade is the moat

    Attested soundings dim as they age until something re-corroborates them. Surveyed soundings never fade, because they re-read live data. One visual rule states the whole competitive argument without a line of copy.

    Concept mockup — not shipped, not built. Every number is illustrative. This is the surface behind finish condition 6 in docs/prd/company-atlas.md: “an admin looks at one page and states what Atlas knows, how much of the company it covers, and what it does not know — and every part of that statement is correct.” Design references: USGS quadrangle index diagrams (which sheets are mapped), admiralty chart soundings (authority as an accumulation of individually dated measurements), and the magenta cautionary overprint.
