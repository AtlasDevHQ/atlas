The company brain stores **reviewed facts** — claims a human approved at the review gate — alongside the raw **episodes** they were drawn from. A claim that was right on Monday can be quietly wrong by Friday, so every fact carries time on two axes and nothing in the brain is ever deleted.

This page covers what those axes mean, the difference between a claim being *replaced* and a claim being *withdrawn*, how to read the brain as it stood at a past instant, and the advisory age signal attached to every fact.

<Callout type="info" title="Where facts sit in the trust order">
  Reviewed facts are authoritative *for their class* and yield to the warehouse in any overlap. They never govern the query surface: the [semantic layer](/semantic-layer) remains the sole authority over what can be queried, what a metric means, and what the agent must clarify first. Every retrieval result carries its tier label, so an answer grounded in a reviewed fact is always distinguishable from one grounded in a warehouse query.
</Callout>

## Two axes of time

Each fact records both when the claim was true in the world and when Atlas learned of it.

| Field | Axis | Meaning |
|-------|------|---------|
| `validFrom` | Valid time | When the claim began holding in the world. `null` means the start was never recorded — not that it started late. |
| `validTo` | Valid time | When the claim stopped holding. Stamped **only** by a human promotion or correction; there is no autonomous supersession. `null` means the claim is still current. |
| `ingestedAt` | Transaction time | When Atlas recorded the claim. Never null. |
| `invalidatedAt` | Tombstone | When the claim was **withdrawn**. `null` for every live fact. |

The two valid-time fields describe the *world*; `ingestedAt` describes *Atlas's knowledge of it*. Keeping them apart is what lets the brain answer "what did we believe on Monday" without pretending Monday's belief was correct.

## Supersession is not retraction

These are different verbs with different consequences, and conflating them is the single most common misreading of the model.

| | **Supersede** | **Retract** |
|---|---|---|
| What it means | The claim *was* true and has since been replaced | The claim should never have been served |
| Field stamped | `validTo` | `invalidatedAt` |
| `status` | Unchanged — the fact stays `published` | Unchanged — retraction is a tombstone, not a demotion |
| Served as current belief | No | No |
| Visible to an `asOf` read inside its old window | **Yes** | **No** — hidden under any `asOf` |
| Edge written | `supersedes`, new claim → old claim | None |

A superseded fact is still `published` and still not retracted; its validity window has simply closed. That is why a current-belief read has to filter on the supersession axis explicitly — a read that only checks review status and the tombstone would serve back the exact belief a human replaced.

**Nothing is ever deleted.** Retraction stamps a tombstone; the row stays stored. This is deliberate — "what we believed on Monday" only answers if Monday's rows are still there.

<Callout type="warn" title="Retraction hides history; supersession preserves it">
  A retracted fact disappears from every fact-serving read, `asOf` included, because hiding history is what the verb is *for*. If you retract a claim you meant to supersede, you lose the ability to explain what the brain believed before the change.

  The one deliberate exception is the [conflict cluster](/guides/brain-conflicts), where a retracted rival stays listed — labelled by `invalidatedAt`, exactly as a superseded rival is labelled there by `validTo` — because it is still why a live claim was contested. That carve-out is also why retraction is not itself content deletion.
</Callout>

## Reading the brain as of a past instant

`searchBrain` takes an optional `asOf` instant. Without it, you get current belief. With it, the fact store switches to a point read: a fact answers when `validFrom <= asOf < validTo`, so a claim a later promotion superseded answers again inside its own window.

Ask the agent directly — "what did we believe about the Q3 revenue definition on 2026-06-01?" — or pass `asOf` on the tool call:

```json
{
  "query": "Q3 revenue definition",
  "include": ["fact"],
  "asOf": "2026-06-01T00:00:00Z"
}
```

`asOf` accepts a date (`2026-06-01`) or a timestamp with an explicit zone (`2026-06-01T09:00:00Z`). A time without a zone is rejected — it would be read in the server's timezone.

A response that carries `asOf` back is **historical**. The agent is instructed to frame every fact in it as "as of *time*", never as current belief.

### Four boundary rules

These are deliberate, and each one exists because the obvious alternative is wrong:

- **Tombstones stay hidden under any `asOf`.** Retraction is the only verb that hides history, and it hides it unconditionally.
- **The grant is frozen; membership is live.** Each fact version carries the immutable access grant it was published with, and the point read evaluates exactly that grant — so Monday's belief is gated by Monday's grant, with no special-casing. The *reader's* memberships, by contrast, always resolve as of now: someone who has since left an audience does not keep historical access through an `asOf` read. Membership is the live half, and it is the revocation path.
- **A `null` `validFrom` is served.** An unrecorded start is not a late start. The current-belief read serves such a fact, so the point read admits it too — otherwise `asOf ≈ now()` would disagree with the default read.
- **A bad `asOf` is refused, never ignored.** A malformed or future `asOf` returns an error rather than silently falling back to current belief, because a caller who asked for history and quietly got today's claims would attribute today's beliefs to Monday.

<Callout type="info" title="What `asOf` does not rewind">
  `asOf` applies to the fact store only. Episodes are append-only evidence of what was *said* and have no validity window; knowledge documents sit outside the truth ordering entirely. An `asOf` read that excludes facts is refused outright rather than echoing `asOf` over current-only content.

  Read-time metadata — the staleness signal, the corroboration count, and the conflict cluster — is always evaluated as of **now**, even on a historical page. Those describe the claim as it stands today (how stale it has since become, what has since come to contradict it), not the belief being reported.
</Callout>

## Staleness is advisory

Every fact carries a read-time `decay` signal. It is derived on read from timestamps the query already holds — there is no stored score, no expiry, and no background job.

| Level | Age of the newest observation |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| `fresh` | Under 30 days |
| `aging` | 30 to 119 days |
| `stale` | 120 days or more |
| `unknown` | No temporal anchor decoded — the honest arm, rather than fabricating an age |

The signal is anchored on the **newest observation** of the claim: the latest event time across the episodes its provenance edges point at, falling back to `validFrom`, then `ingestedAt`. Re-observing a claim adds a provenance edge, so **every corroboration moves the anchor forward** — that is how corroboration slows decay.

**Decay only surfaces; it never demotes.** A published fact never expires, never loses its trust tier, and is never changed by any background job. What the signal is allowed to do:

- The review queue **floats** stale claims for a human's attention. Staleness affects ordering only — a stale fact is surfaced, never filtered out.
- The agent is told to **present a fact's age** ("as of March…") rather than assert a stale claim as current — and explicitly never to discard or overrule a fact because of its age. A `stale` fact is still the reviewed record.

Acting on the signal is a human decision, taken through the [correction verbs](/guides/brain-corrections) or the review gate.

<Callout type="info" title="Age precision and provenance entitlement">
  The newest-observation timestamp is episode data. A reader whose provenance attribution is withheld for a fact receives the coarse `level` only — `ageDays` and `lastObservedAt` come back `null`, because a day-precision age would restate the withheld timestamp as arithmetic. The level itself is still computed from the real anchor.
</Callout>

## Enabling the brain

<WhenSaaS>
Nothing to set up. The company brain is available to every workspace on Atlas Cloud, and the review queue lives in the admin console under **Intelligence → Brain Facts**.
</WhenSaaS>

<WhenSelfHosted>
The brain stores its facts and episodes in the internal database, so a deployment needs `DATABASE_URL`. Access is resolved per reader against the workspace's `member` role, so reviewers reach the queue in the admin console under **Intelligence → Brain Facts**.

Fact **extraction** is off by default and enabled with `ATLAS_BRAIN_EXTRACTION_ENABLED=true`. This is a platform-scoped setting applied at restart. Episodes are stored either way, so turning extraction on later extracts the accumulated backlog rather than losing it.
</WhenSelfHosted>
