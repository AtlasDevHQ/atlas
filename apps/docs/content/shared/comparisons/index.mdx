Atlas is the company facts your AI agents can trust: every one carries its source, its date, and the name of the person who approved it. Open source, runs in your VPC.

Every page here compares on that axis — **who approved it, is it stale, can you self-host it, does it show what it does not know** — and not on feature count. Where a competitor wins on breadth, the page says so. Atlas concedes breadth on purpose: a smaller set of facts you can actually trust is the product, and losing a connector-count comparison to an enterprise search vendor is an expected outcome, not a defect.

Two fields compare themselves to Atlas, and a reader usually arrives from one of them.

## If you are evaluating a company brain

Products that hold what a company knows and hand it to agents. The question that separates them from Atlas is what stands between a fact and an agent acting on it: a model's score, or a person's name.

## If you are evaluating a warehouse analyst

Products that answer questions from tables inside one cloud. They are very good at that, and the tier of Atlas that does the same job — *Surveyed*, true by construction — is not trying to beat them at it. The difference is everything outside the table, and where the thing runs.

Google's equivalent is **Conversational Analytics in BigQuery** (its reusable objects are called *data agents*), generally available since June 2026 for BigQuery and Looker. It sits in the same place as Genie and Cortex Analyst — tables, inside one cloud — and the Genie page's trade-offs read across to it.

## When to Choose What

### Choose Atlas if you need to...

- **Embed data querying in your own product** -- Atlas is designed as a component, not a standalone tool. Script tag, React component, or raw API.
- **Skip infrastructure** -- Sign up at [app.useatlas.dev](https://app.useatlas.dev), connect your database, and start querying. US-hosted, with EU and APAC on request with data residency.
- **Control the AI's reasoning with code** -- The YAML semantic layer lives in your repo, versioned with git, reviewed in PRs. Or use the web editor with autocomplete and version history.
- **An agent that learns** -- `atlas learn` proposes YAML improvements from your query history. The dynamic learning layer captures patterns at runtime with admin review.
- **Support multiple datasources with plugins** -- Connect Postgres, MySQL, BigQuery, Snowflake, ClickHouse, DuckDB, Salesforce, and Elasticsearch/OpenSearch. Browse and install plugins from the marketplace.
- **Reach users where they are** -- 8 integrations via Chat SDK: 6 chat platforms (Slack, Teams, Discord, Telegram, Google Chat, WhatsApp) plus GitHub and Linear.
- **Enterprise compliance** -- SSO/SCIM, custom roles, IP allowlists, approval workflows, PII masking, audit retention, data residency.
- **Embed under MIT** -- The client libraries (`@useatlas/sdk`, `@useatlas/react`) are MIT. Embed them in commercial products with no copyleft concerns.

### Choose WrenAI if you need to...

- **A UI-based semantic modeling experience** -- WrenAI's modeling interface lets non-developers define relationships and metrics visually.
- **A standalone text-to-SQL product** -- WrenAI works well as a dedicated tool for teams that want a BI-like experience with natural language.

### Choose Vanna if you need to...

- **A Python-native workflow** -- Vanna is a Python package. Train it in a notebook, call it from scripts, integrate with existing Python data pipelines.
- **Fully MIT license** -- Permissive license with no copyleft restrictions on any component.
- **Quick prototyping** -- Minimal setup for experimenting with text-to-SQL in a Python environment.

### Choose Metabase if you need to...

- **A full BI platform with dashboards** -- Metabase is a complete business intelligence tool with dashboards, scheduled reports, and team features.
- **A mature, battle-tested product** -- Metabase has been in production at thousands of companies for years.
- **Visual query builder + SQL** -- Not just AI-driven querying, but a visual point-and-click query builder too.
